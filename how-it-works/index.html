How it works

Unbox. Plug in. Ask.

The job is the document they refused to paste. The path is short on purpose.

1

Unbox

The unit arrives imaged. Disk encryption is on. An open-weight model is already on the box. You do not assemble a stack over a weekend.

2

Plug in

Ethernet to your network. Power on. People reach one app on the intranet. No public cloud login. No SSH required to ask a question.

3

Ask

Drop a PDF. Ask about the memo, the model, or the pricing sheet. Prompts, files, and outputs stay on the machine you own.

Privacy / egress

Outbound is off unless you turn it on.

The point of a local box is that a lab never sees the corpus. Egress starts off. Updates are optional. If IT wants a hole, that is a written choice — not a default.

We will not tell you this is “air-gapped by certification.” We will tell you the intended posture: work stays on the LAN; the box does not call a lab to answer a question.

Proof

A status page you can show IT.

Trust is not a paragraph on a marketing site. The box has a local status page so a technical partner can see model state, disk encryption, and whether outbound is on — without taking a sales call as evidence.

What it shows

Loaded model, disk lock, egress on/off, and whether update checks are enabled. Plain language. Screenshot-friendly.

What it is not

Not a public uptime badge. Not a SOC 2 report. Not a claim that nothing can ever leak if someone walks the box out the door.

Who looks at it

The person who will get the call if an associate pastes a client file. Counsel, IT, or the partner who owns the risk.
